CONTROLLED TRAIN-MF CONVERGENCE AND RUNTIME CURVATURE STUDY
Status: COMPLETE. All 36 fits and the completed-output replay audit are recorded; 14/36 selected fits pass the declared KKT tolerance.

The preserved baseline is matlab/reference/mf_robust, version 5.1. It is the
regularized profile-factorization reference, not signed-v2.2. The archived
illustration audited previously recorded solver version 5.0; the new study
does not relabel that saved output or retroactively instrument it.

SOURCE AND INSTRUMENTATION
source_frozen retains the original entry point, all mfrobust helpers and the
dosyv4 preprocessing/regularizer dependency. instrumented contains a separate
DOSY_MF_Runtime entry point. The objective, gradient, NNLS, feasibility tests,
warm stop, multistart selection and SQP settings remain those of reference 5.1.
The experimental control changes the alternating warm cap to 1, 4, 8 or 16.
The original objective-plus-KKT early stop still applies, so caps are not
guaranteed actual iteration counts. All arms then have the same SQP cap 250.

For the specified interior second-density-difference regularizer, the
zero-mass curvature-null direction is v_h=w_h(x_h-weighted_mean(x)). Conditional
injectivity is equivalent to Kv nonzero and full row rank of A. The runtime
checks the dimensionless kernel ratio ||Kv||/(||K||_2 ||v||), amplitude singular
value ratio and operator/mass residuals at tolerance 1e-10. It checks before
each profile block and at reported SQP iterates (including iteration zero),
with logs retained per start.
An unresolved profile-block condition routes that block to the constrained
interior-point least-squares solver, without altering its objective or adding
a penalty. The original objective descent and feasibility checks remain in
force. SQP retains its profiled objective and records unresolved conditions;
it does not require uniqueness of the conditional S block. Numerical KKT
success, conditional-injectivity support and physical identification remain
different statements. The last is never claimed here.
The reduction is specific to this interior second-difference L and positive
lambda_S. It is not a general diagnostic for arbitrary L or lambda_S=0, nor
does support at finitely many iterates establish a uniform coercivity bound.

Five deterministic tests passed: independent amplitude rows, duplicated rows,
a constant acquisition kernel, zero amplitudes, and an actual duplicated-row
profile update that triggered the interior-point fallback and preserved
feasibility and descent. A separate rank1/SNR100/warm8 run of the uninstrumented
frozen solver exactly reproduces the instrumented prediction (relative
difference zero), objective and KKT. The original source tree is unchanged.

EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN
Nine matrices cross generating profile count 1/2/3 and SNR 50/100/200. Profiles
are finite-width lognormal-like masses (log standard deviation 0.14) on the
same 256-node [0.1,15]*1e-9 m^2/s grid, with centers 0.7,2.3,7.1*1e-9 m^2/s.
There are 24 predefined signal-bearing spectral channels spanning 7.2--8.2ppm,
correlated component spectra and overlapping peaks. All channels are retained;
there is no truth-dependent signal selection during inversion. Thirty-two
acquisitions use b=2.2e9*(i/31)^1.7. Every fourth acquisition is external; 24
rows are fitted and eight are reserved. Noise is iid Gaussian with sigma=1/SNR,
with maximum true unattenuated total signal normalized to one. Signed noisy
observations are retained. Configuration, seeds and all inputs were saved
before any inverse fit.

For each input, all four warm caps use identical data, the same greedy first
start and the same seeded random second start, lambda_S=lambda_A=1e-8,
objective tolerance1e-9, KKT tolerance1e-6, inner tolerance1e-10 and inner cap2000.
The generating factor count is supplied deliberately to isolate numerical
optimization. This is not validation of automatic factor selection. Each fit
retains both starts, pre-/post-SQP KKT and objective, profile/amplitude arrays,
training prediction, external prediction error, optimizer flags, all runtime
curvature checks, CPU time and wall time. Numerical failures are retained.
No tolerance, cap or input was changed after inspecting convergence outcomes.

REPRODUCTION

path; run test_runtime_curvature for the five unit tests. Existing inputs and
config are immutable: prepare_mf_experiment refuses to replace config.json.
Use run_mf_case('rank3_snr100',8) for one case, run_mf_batch([1 2]) for ranks1/2,
and run_rank3_queue in independent singleCompThread processes for rank3. The
queue uses atomic claim files to prevent duplicate fits. Existing result MAT
files are retained. Use source_frozen in a fresh MATLAB process for regression
with test_frozen_equivalence('rank1_snr100'). Use audit_completed_outputs after
all36 fits exist. Python aggregate_mf.py summarizes all results, including
failures, and writes the final table/figure; plot_mf_design.py renders the inputs.
For an independent repeat, copy the .m/.py drivers and source_frozen/instrumented
directories to a fresh directory, without config.json, inputs, results or
queue_claims. Run prepare_mf_experiment there, then run_mf_batch([1 2 3]) for a
serial full run. Run audit_completed_outputs and aggregate_mf.py afterwards.
The immutable original directory is retained. Alternatively the queued run
uses no more than three MATLAB -singleCompThread workers. Claim files are
run-coordination state, not evidence that a fit completed; completion requires
the corresponding result MAT/JSON.

LIMITS
Only one noise realization per rank/SNR combination is included. Shared truth
families and paired warm policies are not independent experimental replicates.
Finite-budget KKT diagnostics do not prove factor convergence, global
optimality, a universally sufficient warm count, chemical identities or
experimental DOSY performance. Parallel-process wall times depend on contention;
CPU times and actual iteration counts are also retained. No GPU was used.

FINAL EXECUTED OUTCOMES

Only 1/72 warm-only starts pass the declared KKT tolerance. After the common SQP refinement, the selected fits pass in 3/9, 3/9, 4/9, 4/9 cases for caps 1, 4, 8 and 16, respectively; 48/72 starts terminate at the SQP iteration cap. SQP runs in 71/72 starts; any omitted refinement follows the original KKT rule. Across the nine paired inputs, warm-eight and warm-sixteen selected predictions differ by a median 0.0018 and maximum 0.0104 noise units on external rows, while their largest absolute relative objective difference is 0.0295%. Small predictive or objective differences do not replace the failed KKT checks. Eight warm sweeps are an initialization budget, and neither eight nor sixteen guarantees stationary termination under this finite SQP budget.

The experiment records 14292 before-block/reported-SQP-iterate curvature checks, with 4 numerically unresolved conditions and 4 condition-triggered interior-point fallbacks. The final condition is supported for 72/72 starts. The targeted duplicate-row test exercises the fallback separately. Reevaluation of all 36 saved fits reproduces the stored pre-SQP/final objectives, KKT residuals and training-row predictions, checks common initializations and feasibility, and verifies monotonicity of saved objective histories at the original tolerance. This is a fresh-process audit of saved states, not a second execution of the optimizer or a reevaluation of every historical iterate. Runtime support and finite-budget stationarity are thus empirically different diagnostics. The four flagged checks arise in the same second initialization of the rank-three/SNR-200 input, once per warm cap; they are not four independent data realizations.



Total summed CPU time: 10957.0 s. Total serial sum of per-fit wall times: 11518.1 s. These are not parallel-study elapsed time.

The S-block least-squares solves returned 0 nonpositive optimizer flags. All returned starts remain feasible in the independent replay audit.
